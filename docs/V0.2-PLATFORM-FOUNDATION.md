# v0.2.0 — Platform Foundation

`v0.1.0` is the immutable OTA proof-of-concept baseline. The purpose of
`v0.2.0` is to turn that hardware bring-up into a maintainable firmware
platform before broad feature development begins.

## Release status

`v0.2.0` was tagged on 2026-07-26 with two of the eight acceptance criteria
below still open. They are **carried forward into `v0.3.0` unchanged**, not
relaxed to match what shipped — a target that moves whenever it is missed
stops being a target.

| # | Criterion | `v0.2.0` |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | static RAM at or below 44 KiB, 20 KiB left for stack | open — 45,332 B and 20,196 B |
| 2 | one navigation model across watchface, launcher, quick settings, settings | open — launcher and quick settings do not exist |
| 3 | pairing and DFU modals cannot be overwritten | met |
| 4 | sleep/wake restores the active screen | met |
| 5 | settings survive reboot | met |
| 6 | complete Gadgetbridge OTA flow on hardware | met |
| 7 | red-logo recovery documented and usable | met |
| 8 | CI formatting, Clippy, host tests, release builds, budgets | met |

**Criterion 1 was met in `v0.2.1`**: narrowing the transition stripe to 12
pixels brought static RAM to 43,412 B with 22,116 B left for stack growth, and
CI now enforces the targets rather than the hard limits. That release also fixed
a swipe consuming its own touch, so a gesture no longer activates the control
under the finger.

Criterion 2 is half closed since then: the application launcher exists, opened
by swiping up, with settings and firmware as tiles and the provisional routes
removed. Quick settings on the left gesture is what remains.

Milestone A's measurement item is satisfied by the memory table in
[`releases/v0.2.0.md`](releases/v0.2.0.md), which records the four largest
static allocations.

Two things shipped that the criteria did not ask for, and the remaining work
leans on both: the side button became the back button once a software restart
existed to replace it as the recovery path, and the paginated list model the
launcher and the settings pages are built from.

## Product direction

PineForge is an embedded firmware platform, not a general-purpose operating
system. Embassy schedules single-owner hardware tasks; services own product
policy; a hardware-independent application model owns navigation; screens
render state without directly accessing peripherals.

The first product slice is:

```text
Watchface
    ├── Application launcher  (up)
    │       └── Settings
    │               └── Firmware and recovery
    ├── Notifications         (down, deferred)
    └── Quick settings        (left)
```

Settings hang under the launcher rather than off a gesture of their own: they
are visited rarely, and the launcher is where an application is chosen.

Notifications and health applications follow only after this slice works
through navigation, persistence, sleep/wake, and OTA. The gesture that will
open them is fixed now so it does not have to be relearned later.

## Navigation contract

- The watchface is the root and cannot be popped.
- A consistent back gesture pops one screen: a screen is left by the reverse
  of the gesture that opened it.

From the watchface, three directions lead somewhere and the fourth is the way
back out of everything:

| Gesture | Destination | Leaves by | The screen's own paging |
|---|---|---|---|
| up | application launcher | down | horizontal |
| down | notifications | up | horizontal |
| left | quick settings | right | one page, none |
| right | — | — | reserved for back |

Pulling notifications down is the habit people arrive with, so it is worth a
direction of its own even though notifications themselves are deferred:
reserving it now costs nothing, and moving it later costs the user a relearn.

The paging column is not decoration. The axis of the entry gesture belongs to
navigation on that screen, so a paginated list has to page on the other one.
That makes the launcher a grid of tile pages rather than a vertical menu, and
it is the reason the directions are decided before the screens are built.
Screens reached by a tap have no entry gesture and are left by the side button
or an on-screen control.
- The side button pops one screen too, so a screen reached by a tap can be
  left without an on-screen control. It is an addition to the back gesture,
  not a replacement for it.
- A press on a sleeping watch wakes it and does nothing else.
- Holding the side button for two seconds resets the watch. The reset must
  stay reachable without help from the display task, and a software restart on
  the firmware screen exists so the button is not the only reboot path.
- Holding the button during boot still reaches the bootloader's minimal
  recovery image; that path is bootloader-side and unaffected by firmware.
- Pairing, DFU, confirmation, and fatal recovery prompts are system modals
  above the screen stack.
- A modal consumes the input that dismisses it; that input never also
  navigates the underlying screen.
- A gesture consumes the touch it was recognized from. The minimum swipe
  distance fits inside one menu row, so the release that ends a swipe would
  otherwise activate the control the finger started on.

A screen opened by a tile has no entry gesture of its own, so it borrows the
default one and is left by the reverse of it — the same direction that leaves
everything else, and the same one the side button performs. The provisional
routes that kept settings and firmware reachable before the launcher existed are
gone: both are tiles now.

## Lifecycle contract

Each screen has:

- persistent model state owned outside rendering where appropriate;
- an explicit enter/leave boundary for starting and stopping services;
- hardware-independent event handling;
- full redraw after wake;
- bounded partial redraw while active;
- no direct ownership of BLE, sensors, flash, or display hardware.

Background services continue independently of the visible screen unless an
explicit power or privacy policy suspends them.

## Memory budgets

Production targets:

| Resource | Hard CI limit | v0.2 target |
|---|---:|---:|
| Static RAM | 48 KiB | no more than 44 KiB |
| Runtime/stack reserve | 16 KiB minimum | 20 KiB minimum |
| Flash | 384 KiB | no more than 360 KiB |

Subsystem guidance:

- display transition scratch: no more than 8 KiB;
- BLE packet pool: change only with an end-to-end OTA hardware test;
- DFU queue: change only with an end-to-end OTA hardware test;
- new channels must document element size and capacity;
- large values must not remain live across an `await` without a measured need.

The transition stripe was narrowed twice, because it is the only large
allocation whose size is a pure trade against render time rather than against a
validated protocol floor:

| Stripe | Buffer | Stripes per transition |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 24 px | 11.25 KiB | 10 |
| 16 px | 7.5 KiB | 15 |
| 12 px | 5.6 KiB | 20 |

The screen is composed once per stripe, so each step buys RAM with clipped full
draws. 12 pixels divides the 240-pixel span evenly and brings production static
RAM to 42.4 KiB with 21.6 KiB left for stack growth, which is what closed
acceptance criterion 1. CI now enforces these targets rather than the hard slot
limits. 8 pixels remains available if a later feature needs the space more than
the animation needs the speed.

Slide transitions are gated by the `ui-animations` Cargo feature and are part
of the normal production default. Memory experiments can disable default
features and re-enable only BLE, without changing the navigation contract:

```bash
cargo build --release --no-default-features --features ble
```

`scripts/build-dfu.sh` builds this production profile by default. Feature
variants are opt-in through `PINEFORGE_FEATURES` and must be labeled as
diagnostic artifacts; routine OTA validation always uses the production image.

## Milestone work

### A. Foundation and measurement

- preserve `v0.1.0` tag and release unchanged;
- record production and diagnostics section sizes in CI;
- reduce production static RAM below 44 KiB where hardware behavior permits;
- document the largest static allocations and task futures;
- retain successful format, Clippy, host-test, flash-budget, and RAM-budget
  gates.

Open at `v0.2.0`: the RAM reduction, and CI records flash and RAM totals rather
than per-section sizes.

### B. Navigation shell

- add an application launcher screen;
- add a quick-settings screen;
- make navigation rules explicit in the pure state crate;
- centralize system-modal ownership;
- add host tests for root, push, back, modal dismissal, and sleep/wake redraw.

Open at `v0.2.0`: both screens. Navigation rules, modal ownership and their host
tests are in place; the sleep/wake redraw is exercised on hardware but lives in
the display task and has no host test.

### C. Product slice

- polished watchface;
- launcher to settings and back;
- brightness and timeout controls with persistence;
- battery and BLE status;
- firmware confirmation, OTA progress, failure, and recovery UI;
- complete hardware test covering boot, navigation, sleep/wake, settings
  persistence, OTA, and recovery.

Open at `v0.2.0`: the launcher route into settings, and the watchface polish,
which waits for the UI design plan rather than being done twice. Boot, OTA,
settings persistence and recovery are hardware-tested; navigation and sleep/wake
have been exercised but not against a written checklist.

## Acceptance criteria

These are the criteria as written for the milestone; see
[Release status](#release-status) for which ones `v0.2.0` shipped with.

`v0.2.0` is complete when:

1. production static RAM is at or below 44 KiB with at least 20 KiB left for
   runtime stack growth;
2. the watchface, launcher, quick settings, and settings use one documented
   navigation model;
3. pairing and DFU modals cannot be overwritten by background events;
4. sleep/wake restores the active screen correctly;
5. settings survive reboot;
6. the complete Gadgetbridge OTA flow still reaches 100%, validates, and
   boots the staged image on hardware;
7. the red-logo minimal InfiniTime recovery path remains documented and
   usable;
8. CI passes formatting, strict Clippy, host tests, release builds, and size
   budgets.

## Explicitly deferred

- final visual branding and pixel-perfect component styling;
- notifications;
- alarms and timers;
- production heart-rate algorithms;
- weather and music control;
- external resource packs;
- aggressive BLE/DFU buffer reductions without hardware evidence.
