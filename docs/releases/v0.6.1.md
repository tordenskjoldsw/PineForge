# PineForge v0.6.1 - The Screen Moves

> [!CAUTION]
> Flashing may leave a sealed PineTime unusable or require recovery. A sealed watch has no exposed SWD pads, so an image that neither boots nor reaches the bootloader's recovery path can only be waited out until the battery runs flat. Keep an official InfiniTime DFU package available and read the installation and recovery instructions before flashing.

[v0.6.0](v0.6.0.md) put time under the watch's own control. This release is about two things the watch could not do before: turn its radio off and leave it off, and move a screen instead of redrawing one.

Settings survive the update. The stored record grew a field and its version went from 5 to 6, and every version from 1 on migrates when it is read, so no settings reset is needed. BLE bonds are untouched and re-pairing is not required.

## A Bluetooth application, and an OFF that stays off

The new **BLUETOOTH** application carries one full-width ENABLE or DISABLE button. Pressing it changes the desired state as soon as the button is released; the status corner keeps its rune until the BLE task reports that the radio is actually down, so the corner reports what is, not what was asked for. OFF clears that corner cell and draws no replacement rune in its place.

The setting is persistent. A watch switched off stays off across a reboot rather than quietly advertising again, which is the entire point of having the control: a radio that returns on its own is not a radio you can turn off. Switching back on restarts advertising, and Gadgetbridge reconnects without re-pairing.

During a firmware transfer the button is disabled. Disconnecting the transport that is installing the running image is not a valid setting change.

This was exercised on the known sealed PineTime as the `0.6.1+1` development image: disabling disconnects the phone and clears the rune, OFF survives a reboot, enabling restarts advertising and the phone reconnects without re-pairing, and ON survives a reboot too. Settings migration, the DFU lockout and the rendering additionally pass host tests.

## Vertical transitions became real movement

Until now a screen change was a reveal: the incoming screen appeared strip by strip while nothing on the panel moved. Vertically it now slides, and the panel's own controller does the sliding.

The ST7789 holds 240x320 pixels and shows a 240-row window of them, and which rows are shown is a register. Each step of a transition composes one 12-row band of the incoming screen into rows the panel is not showing, then moves the window by exactly that band, so the band just written is the strip the move uncovers. The screen being left is carried off without a single one of its pixels being sent a second time.

That makes the whole movement cost one frame - the same 240x240 an ordinary full redraw costs - rather than one frame per step, which is why an animation is possible at all on a bus that needs 115 ms for a full panel. The technique is the same one `InfiniTime` uses for its vertical transitions.

The price is that the window does not return to where it started: after a slide, screen row 0 is some other row of frame memory, permanently. The translation lives in the display task's panel wrapper, is applied to every later draw and splits any run that falls off the end of memory, and nothing above that wrapper knows it happens.

Horizontally there is no such register, so a screen entered across the panel is still revealed strip by strip. That is the hardware's answer, not a preference.

Watchface to launcher, watchface to notifications and the way back out of both slide, as does every page turn on a vertically paged screen: the launcher, the settings menus, the firmware screen and the setting pickers.

## The launcher pages the way it opens

The launcher used to page sideways, because a screen is left by the reverse of the gesture that opened it and the vertical axis was therefore spoken for. That rule is what kept its pages off the only axis the panel can move on.

It now pages vertically. Up carries on into the next page, down comes back, and from the first page down is the watchface again - the same one gesture the whole way. The exception is narrow by construction: a paged screen declares each gesture before navigation sees it, claiming the swipe only while it has a page in that direction and handing it back at the ends, where it becomes the way out. A screen cannot swallow its own exit.

The page rail follows the axis it reports on, so it now stands upright to the right of the tiles, and the grid is centred in what the status corner leaves rather than sitting above an empty strip.

Both were exercised on the known sealed PineTime as the `0.6.1+2` development image, whose firmware source is identical to this release.

## Known limits

- Tearing at the boundary between writing a band and moving the window has not been measured, only looked at. The step is 12 rows because that is the existing band buffer; `InfiniTime` moves 4 and a finer step costs composition time rather than bus time.
- Browsing notifications still redraws the text inside the card rather than sliding the card, which is deliberate: that path repaints a text field where a slide would repaint a frame.
- Production flash is now within 2,516 bytes of its design budget. The next feature of any size will have to argue for the budget rather than assume it.
- Step counts and heart-rate readings have still not been compared with reference instruments.
- Battery life and current consumption remain unmeasured.
- Music recovery after sleep, Bluetooth disconnect/reconnect and switching media applications mid-track remain untested.
- The release has run on one sealed PineTime, one bootloader version and one known phone/Gadgetbridge setup.
- Everything continues to depend on the stock InfiniTime MCUBoot 1.0.1 layout and recovery image.

See [tested configurations](../TESTED-CONFIGURATIONS.md) for the exact boundary of the hardware evidence.

## Memory

| Resource | v0.6.0 | v0.6.1 | Budget |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Flash | 431,140 B | 439,852 B | 442,368 B |
| Static RAM | 48,604 B | 48,788 B | 49,152 B |

The budgets did not move. Flash grew by 8,712 bytes across the two features, and the sliding transition accounts for 5,156 of them: the panel wrapper has to translate and, where necessary, split every drawing operation the watch performs. Static RAM grew by 184 bytes and none of it belongs to the animation, which reuses the 5.6 KiB band buffer that was already there.

## Installation

1. Fully charge the watch.
2. Keep a known-good official InfiniTime DFU ZIP on the phone.
3. Read `GETTING-STARTED.md` and the sealed-device testing guide.
4. Download `pineforge-mcuboot-app-dfu-0.6.1.zip` from this release.
5. Verify its SHA-256 checksum against the value below.
6. Open the ZIP with Gadgetbridge's firmware installer.
7. Keep the phone and watch close together until validation and reboot finish.

Do not extract the DFU ZIP before giving it to Gadgetbridge.

## Recovery

- Before PineForge is confirmed, a restart lets MCUBoot roll back to the previous image. Restart is available from the firmware screen or by holding the side button for two seconds.
- To start the bootloader's minimal InfiniTime recovery image, hold the physical side button during boot until the boot logo turns red.
- Connect to that recovery image over Bluetooth and install a known-good InfiniTime or PineForge DFU ZIP.
