# PineForge v0.5.0 - Music and System Status

> [!CAUTION]
> Flashing may leave a sealed PineTime unusable or require recovery. A sealed watch has no exposed SWD pads, so an image that neither boots nor reaches the bootloader's recovery path can only be waited out until the battery runs flat. Keep an official InfiniTime DFU package available and read the installation and recovery instructions before flashing.

[v0.4.0](v0.4.0.md) established the FORGE visual language and measured how the panel should be driven. This release gives that language another daily-use application and gives a sealed watch somewhere to report what is happening underneath it.

Settings and BLE bonds survive the update: the stored formats did not change, so installing v0.5.0 requires neither a settings reset nor re-pairing.

## Music on the wrist

The new **Music** application speaks the music service used by InfiniTime and Gadgetbridge. It shows title and artist, elapsed time over a progress bar, and previous, play/pause and next controls. Swiping up and down changes the phone's volume.

The elapsed clock is anchored to the uptime at which the phone reported its position instead of being incremented once a second. That distinction matters on a watch whose panel and display task sleep: after waking, the elapsed time catches up from one subtraction instead of falling behind by the duration of every sleep.

Music is also the first PineForge event path that runs outward. Sensor readings and phone writes used to end at the UI; a transport press now travels back through a bounded channel to BLE and becomes the notified control byte Gadgetbridge expects. A blocked radio cannot block the display task: when the queue is full, the request is dropped and the button remains responsive.

Disconnected controls are visibly disabled. Track state is assembled as one retained record rather than sent as a burst of title, artist and timing events, so opening the app after a change cannot combine the new title with stale metadata. Repaints are bounded as well: a track change redraws the two text lines and clock, while a one-second tick redraws only the numerals that changed.

On the known Gadgetbridge setup, title and artist display correctly; previous and next, pause and resume, and volume in both directions have all been exercised on hardware.

## About became a system-status view

About now has three vertically paged screens:

1. **About** - release, commit, commit date and the current `MCUBOOT` bootloader target.
2. **Hardware** - touch-controller state, detected motion and pulse sensors, and the external flash JEDEC ID.
3. **System** - trial or confirmed image state, BLE state, stack high-water mark and the latest retained fault.

These values are typed service events, not strings recovered from debug logs. The screen retains them while another application is open and while the panel sleeps, which is important because most probes happen only once during boot. The stack report retries if the UI queue is full during the initial burst of hardware results rather than losing the only reading.

All three pages have been flashed and displayed on the known sealed PineTime. Alternative sensor and flash variants, and the uncommon failure values that require deliberately broken hardware or transfers, have not been exercised.

## The pulse screen joined FORGE

The pulse application's heart-rate value now uses the same rectangle-built numerals as the FORGE clock and steps application. An absent reading is three unlit places rather than `000`, and the colour still distinguishes a measurement in progress from a settled result.

## Rendering work that stayed measured

The release includes a targeted speed build for the crates that actually rasterise the interface. Compiling only those crates for speed cost 5,956 bytes of flash and no static RAM; on the measured navigation it reduced a forward transition from 296 ms to 283 ms and a backward transition from 219 ms to 213 ms.

Broader experiments were removed when their measurements did not justify them. The SPI path already moved a frame in 121 ms against a theoretical 115.2 ms floor, so a new chained-DMA driver was not written. Skipping clipped glyph rows changed no measured transition time and was reverted instead of leaving complexity in the font renderer.

## Known limits

- Music sleep recovery, Bluetooth disconnect/reconnect and switching media applications mid-track remain untested.
- The system-status pages have run on one sealed PineTime. This does not validate other hardware revisions, sensor variants or flash chips.
- Step counts and heart-rate readings have still not been compared with reference instruments.
- Battery life and current consumption remain unmeasured.
- The latest-fault field retains one fault, not a persistent history across reboots.
- Everything continues to depend on the stock InfiniTime MCUBoot 1.0.1 layout and recovery image.

See [tested configurations](../TESTED-CONFIGURATIONS.md) for the exact boundary of the hardware evidence.

The production RAM budget deliberately leaves the rest of the nRF52832 RAM region for stack growth. About exposes the observed high-water mark on the watch so this reserve can be checked without RTT.

## Installation

1. Fully charge the watch.
2. Keep a known-good official InfiniTime DFU ZIP on the phone.
3. Read `GETTING-STARTED.md` and the sealed-device testing guide.
4. Download `pineforge-mcuboot-app-dfu-0.5.0.zip` from this release.
5. Verify its SHA-256 checksum against the value below.
6. Open the ZIP with Gadgetbridge's firmware installer.
7. Keep the phone and watch close together until validation and reboot finish.

Do not extract the DFU ZIP before giving it to Gadgetbridge.

## Recovery

- Before PineForge is confirmed, a restart lets MCUBoot roll back to the previous image. Restart is available from the firmware screen or by holding the side button for two seconds.
- To start the bootloader's minimal InfiniTime recovery image, hold the physical side button during boot until the boot logo turns red.
- Connect to that recovery image over Bluetooth and install a known-good InfiniTime or PineForge DFU ZIP.

The annotated `v0.5.0` tag points at the later documentation commit that records these notes; no firmware source changed between the two commits.

The firmware embeds its source commit, so reproducing the published application image requires checking out `bcfcfd2712a712e28911ddca7a9f19105cdb682f`, not the tag: building the later tag embeds that later commit ID and therefore changes the image bytes. The archive around the image is not byte-reproducible either because `adafruit-nrfutil` records file timestamps; verify the ZIP against the published asset and the image against a rebuild of the named build commit.
