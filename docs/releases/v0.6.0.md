# PineForge v0.6.0 - Time Under Control

> [!CAUTION]
> Flashing may leave a sealed PineTime unusable or require recovery. A sealed watch has no exposed SWD pads, so an image that neither boots nor reaches the bootloader's recovery path can only be waited out until the battery runs flat. Keep an official InfiniTime DFU package available and read the installation and recovery instructions before flashing.

[v0.5.0](v0.5.0.md) put music controls on the wrist and made About somewhere the watch could explain itself. This release concentrates on time: measuring it, counting it down, and setting it without requiring a phone.

Settings and BLE bonds survive the update. Their stored formats did not change, so installing v0.6.0 requires neither a settings reset nor re-pairing. The new clock journal uses two previously unused sectors inside the confirmed PineForge data area.

## A stopwatch in the FORGE language

The new **Stopwatch** application shows minutes, seconds and tenths in the same rectangle-built numerals as the FORGE watchface. START becomes PAUSE while it runs and RESUME while paused; RESET is available only when a run is stopped.

Elapsed time is derived from a monotonic start anchor rather than advanced by repaint ticks. Leaving the application or putting the display to sleep therefore does not lose time, and a delayed draw catches up from one subtraction instead of replaying every missed tenth.

The stopwatch has been exercised on the known sealed PineTime: start, pause, resume, reset, navigation away and display sleep all work.

## A countdown that becomes a system alarm

The new **Timer** application selects a duration from one to 99 minutes, then supports pause, resume and cancel. Like the stopwatch, it stores a monotonic deadline rather than decrementing state once per second.

That deadline remains scheduled even when another application is open or the panel is asleep. Reaching zero wakes the display, raises a full-screen alarm above the navigation stack and starts an approximately five-second repeating vibration. Touch, swipe or the side button acknowledges the alarm and cancels the motor immediately.

Pairing, firmware transfer and first-boot storage formatting retain their higher modal priority. If one of them is already visible when the timer expires, the alarm remains pending and appears when the higher-priority flow closes rather than being lost behind it.

The countdown and alarm path work on hardware. The final build also carries the larger bold phase labels, unified turquoise normal-state text and longer cancellable vibration developed from that test.

## TIME and DATE work without Bluetooth

Two separate applications now edit the wall clock directly:

- **TIME** selects hour or minute.
- **DATE** selects year, month or day.

The active field is turquoise in the large segmented value. Minus and plus adjust it, NEXT advances to the following field, and APPLY updates the running system clock immediately. Calendar validation happens in the state model: leap years are handled, invalid month/day combinations cannot be applied, and changing year or month clamps the day to the destination month's valid range.

Manual values do not disable phone synchronization. A later valid Current Time write from Gadgetbridge remains authoritative and replaces the manual or restored reference; merely establishing a BLE connection changes nothing by itself.

Both editors, APPLY, restoration across reboots and subsequent replacement by phone time have been tested on the known watch.

## A power-loss-tolerant clock journal

The external flash now reserves two 4 KiB sectors for wall-clock checkpoints. Each sector holds 128 fixed-size records with a complete date and time, wrapping sequence number, version, magic and CRC32. A manual change or valid phone update appends immediately, and a known clock receives an hourly checkpoint. Duplicate snapshots cost no write.

Boot scans both sectors and chooses the newest valid record. A partial final write is rejected by its CRC. When the active sector fills, the alternate sector is erased before its first new record, leaving the previous valid sector intact throughout rotation. The storage task remains the only owner of the flash, and it does not touch this journal until the running image is confirmed and PineForge storage is ready.

The PineTime has no battery-backed real-time clock in this design. A checkpoint preserves the last calendar value, not the time spent without power: after a reboot it resumes from the stored value until Gadgetbridge supplies a fresh one.

## Partial drawing down to the changed digit

TIME and DATE initially repainted their whole value row and every control after an adjustment. The final implementation follows the timer's retained-rendering model instead. A normal plus or minus redraws only the numeral cells whose digits changed and the button that was touched. NEXT additionally redraws the old and new field groups whose colours changed, the field label, and the two controls whose labels changed. The title, separators, APPLY and untouched digits remain standing on the panel.

Host probes assert those boundaries directly: a simple increment in either editor paints exactly one changed numeral cell and one button. The selective result has also been checked on hardware.

The same typography pass gives timer, stopwatch, steps and pulse one native bold FORGE instrument face while ordinary guidance stays in the shared regular UI face. PineForge remains strict `no_std`, with no heap and no full-screen framebuffer.

## Known limits

- A powered-off interval cannot be recovered without a battery-backed RTC. Phone synchronization corrects the restored checkpoint after reconnecting.
- Stopwatch controls and sleep behaviour work on hardware, but an extended run has not been compared against a reference clock.
- Step counts and heart-rate readings have still not been compared with reference instruments.
- Battery life and current consumption remain unmeasured.
- Music recovery after sleep, Bluetooth disconnect/reconnect and switching media applications mid-track remain untested.
- The release has run on one sealed PineTime, one bootloader version and one known phone/Gadgetbridge setup.
- Everything continues to depend on the stock InfiniTime MCUBoot 1.0.1 layout and recovery image.

See [tested configurations](../TESTED-CONFIGURATIONS.md) for the exact boundary of the hardware evidence.

## Memory

| Resource | v0.6.0 | Budget |
|---|---:|---:|
| Flash | 431,140 B | 442,368 B |
| Static RAM | 48,604 B | 49,152 B |

The production flash target was deliberately raised from 420 KiB to 432 KiB for the two clock editors and their power-loss-tolerant journal. It remains a design budget rather than the 475,104-byte physical image ceiling. The RAM budget did not move and still reserves the remainder of the nRF52832 RAM region for stack growth.

## Installation

1. Fully charge the watch.
2. Keep a known-good official InfiniTime DFU ZIP on the phone.
3. Read `GETTING-STARTED.md` and the sealed-device testing guide.
4. Download `pineforge-mcuboot-app-dfu-0.6.0.zip` from this release.
5. Verify its SHA-256 checksum against the value below.
6. Open the ZIP with Gadgetbridge's firmware installer.
7. Keep the phone and watch close together until validation and reboot finish.

Do not extract the DFU ZIP before giving it to Gadgetbridge.

## Recovery

- Before PineForge is confirmed, a restart lets MCUBoot roll back to the previous image. Restart is available from the firmware screen or by holding the side button for two seconds.
- To start the bootloader's minimal InfiniTime recovery image, hold the physical side button during boot until the boot logo turns red.
- Connect to that recovery image over Bluetooth and install a known-good InfiniTime or PineForge DFU ZIP.
